§00

Reader's key

Seven terms the rest of the page uses constantly, in plain words. Skip if you live in this literature.

RLVR

RL with verifiable rewards: the model is graded by a checker — the tests pass, the answer matches — instead of by human preference. It's the only RL flavor on this page.

GRPO group

The same task is attempted ~8–32 times, and each attempt is scored against the group's average. A group where everything fails (or everything succeeds) has no contrast, so it teaches nothing — half the tricks in §05 exist to avoid that.

pass@1 vs pass@k

Score on the first try vs best-of-k tries. RL reliably raises pass@1 (the model becomes dependable at what it could already occasionally do); raising pass@k — genuinely new capability — is rarer and needs special machinery.

Total vs active params

A mixture-of-experts model stores all its experts (total) but runs only a few per token (active). Roughly: total is how much it knows, active is how much thinking each token gets. Snowball is 67B total, 2B active.

Scaffold / harness

The agent wrapper around the model — its tools, prompts, and retry logic. Swapping it can move a benchmark score 2–4×, so scores only compare fairly within one harness.

TIS

Truncated importance sampling: a correction used when the text was generated by a slightly different version of the model than the one being updated — routine in modern RL stacks, and the thing our bug report #438 is about.

On-policy distillation

The student writes its own attempts and a stronger teacher grades every token of them. Denser feedback than RL's single end-of-episode reward, and typically 5–12× cheaper for the same gain.

§01

The global trend

Four findings that hold across every sweep. Everything downstream — the map, the matchups, the targets — is an instance of one of these.

The recipe converged, and it's boring on purpose

Three independent env-scaling papers and three frontier reports landed on the same skeleton: synthesize verified training environments at scale, keep task difficulty at the model's frontier, run simple outcome-only RL on top, and spend the cleverness on making the rollout engine and the trainer numerically agree. Nobody is winning with algorithm novelty; the winners state the boring algorithm as a finding.

Tmax · SETA · Endless Terminals · GLM-5

SFT lifts, RL crosses

The big raw score lift from a weak base is SFT/distillation (Nemotron-Terminal 32B: 3.4→27.4 TB2.0, pure SFT). RL is the segment that pushes a model past bigger frontier models rather than toward them (TMax-27B: 40.5→44.9 crosses Kimi-K2.5 and MiniMax-M2.7). And they compose: RL on a strong distilled base still adds +16.4 AIME25.

Nemotron-Terminal · Tmax · AceReason 1.1

Two different games: total vs active params

Agentic wins are total-param upsets; competition-math wins are active-param upsets — 0.7B active reaches 89 AIME26, and 3B-active models beat 22B-active on AIME while losing GPQA to the same models. GPQA tracks pretraining and never appears in the RL win column.

ZAYA1 · Qwen3-30B-A3B · Cascade-2

Everyone stages now

Every serious 2026 pipeline is a cascade with distillation repair at stage boundaries (GLM-5, Cascade-2, ZAYA1) or parallel specialists consolidated by on-policy distillation (DeepSeek-V4, Qwen3-Coder-Next). Single-stage RL survives only where the environment pipeline is the contribution. OT-Agent v1's RL was a single-stage RLOO pass — the staged version is unclaimed territory for us.

§05

Technique index

The load-bearing tricks of the last six months, grouped by what they fix. Each card is one candidate ingredient for the Snowball recipe.

MoE-RL stability — prerequisites, not options

Rollout routing replay

MoE RL often collapses because the generation engine and the trainer pick different experts for the same token — the two sides are literally running different networks. The fix: record which experts fired during generation and replay exactly those in the training pass. Beats TIS-style corrections outright.

R3 · PR2

Consistency engineering

Tiny bookkeeping differences between the rollout engine and the trainer — re-tokenizing text, GPU kernels that don't sort ties the same way twice — are enough to sink a run. GLM-5's checklist: pass tokens (never re-tokenized text) between engines, use deterministic kernels, freeze the router during RL, drop stale rollouts. One non-deterministic operation alone collapsed their runs within steps.

GLM-5 · Qwen practices

TIS is not enough for MoE

The standard off-policy patch turns out too weak here: with sequence-level TIS, being just two update-steps stale is enough to collapse a long multi-turn run. Router-aware corrections or full routing replay are needed instead. House finding: our own TIS ratios are additionally corrupted by a temperature/log-prob mismatch — MarinSkyRL #438.

VCPO · RSPO

Entropy as a live dial

Watch the policy's entropy during training and it tells you which side of the exploration sweet spot you're on: falling fast means exploration is failing and being punished into collapse (sampling too hot), pinned low means the model never explores (too cold), and a gentle rise means new solutions are being found and kept. Picking the right constant was worth ~3 AIME points in AceReason (0.85 beat both 0.6 and 1.0).

Cui et al. · AceReason

Data & curriculum — where the wins actually come from

Difficulty evolution against the live model

A task the model always solves teaches nothing, and so does one it always fails — the learning signal lives in between. SETA-Evol makes the task generator track the model: as it improves, operators make tasks harder (or easier) to keep every group informative. The single clearest environment-side lever.

SETA

Pass-rate control at ~50%

Same goal, sampler-side: the learning signal peaks when the model succeeds about half the time. Prefix sampling gets a skewed task there without a teacher — seed a known-good beginning into a task the model always fails (now it sometimes succeeds), or a bad beginning into one it always solves. Worth 1.5–2× in wall-clock.

2605.05112

Teacher hints at the 0/k boundary

For problems the student fails on every single attempt, no amount of RL helps — there is no success to reinforce. The fix: give teacher hints on exactly those problems until some attempts succeed, then let RL consolidate without the hint. The one recipe with receipts for expanding capability (pass@256 +10.3 over vanilla RL), and it's literally distillation and RL composing per-example.

2606.22317

Verifier diversification

If every task is graded the same way, the model learns the grader's quirks instead of the skill. Tmax's cheap defense: vary how completion is checked across tasks, and use graded (not just pass/fail) rewards where the task allows it.

Tmax

Curated-small over crawled-large

More RL data isn't better data: selecting the best ~20% of a dataset (by difficulty fit, diversity, and influence) and spending the savings on more attempts per problem matches full-data training at about half the compute.

DEPO · CoScale-RL

Saturation gating

Why RL narrows a model: once a problem is solved, continuing to train on it only concentrates probability on the one solution the sampler favors — killing the diversity that pass@k measures. The fix is data-side and cheap: retire solved problems from the mix and push updates toward unsolved ones, and the pass@k spread stays open.

BBG

Objectives & transfer

Format-gated composite rewards

How do you make a model adopt one habit — say, checking its work before submitting — without changing anything else? Multiply the reward from three checkable parts: did the required structure appear (yes/no gate), was the answer right, and was it efficient. Each part can be turned off to prove it matters. Result: +14.5 LiveCodeBench while using fewer tokens. The template for our behavior recipe cards.

ReflexiCoder

On-policy distillation, gated

Let the student attempt tasks and have the teacher grade every token — much denser feedback than RL's one reward per episode, at 5–12× less compute. The 2026 catch: in multi-turn agent settings, applying teacher pressure uniformly backfires; it has to be gated to the steps where the student actually went wrong, or used only to steer exploration while a verifiable reward stays the objective.

TGPO · SOD · Rethinking OPD

RL deltas as teachers

What RL changed in a model is itself reusable: take a checkpoint from before and after an RL run, and the difference between their token probabilities acts as a dense reward for training a new student — transferring the improvement without re-running the RL. Every RL run we've already paid for becomes an asset.

Direct-OPD

Prompt-opt as baseline and curriculum

GEPA showed a few hundred rollouts of automated prompt search can capture much of what RL buys, so any RL claim now needs an optimized-prompt baseline to be credible (it's an ICLR'26 oral). P²O turns that into a training ingredient: when a task group is unlearnable (all attempts fail), evolve a prompt that makes it solvable, distill that behavior into the weights, and continue RL. Nobody has run this on terminal agents with an MoE student.

GEPA · P²O · OPSD law

Collateral report card

RL runs change more than the target skill, and almost nobody measures what else moved. The ingredients now exist: how far the model drifted from its pre-RL self predicts forgetting (RL's Razor); the pass@k spread shows whether diversity collapsed; automated diffing of generations surfaces unexpected behavior changes. Nobody has bundled them into a standard per-run audit — we can be first, with tooling we already have.

RL's Razor · side-effect audits

Cross-stage distillation repair

In a staged pipeline, each RL stage quietly damages the domains it isn't training — the code stage erodes math, and so on. The repair that makes cascades viable: between stages, briefly distill from whichever earlier checkpoint was strongest in each damaged domain. Both GLM-5 and Cascade-2 treat this as load-bearing, not optional.

What post-training alone is worth

Qwen shipped two models from the same pretrained base: Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct, given a plain instruction post-train, and Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Thinking, given a reasoning post-train. Pretraining is held identical, so the distance between them is attributable to post-training alone — the same lever an RL recipe pulls. It is the cleanest published measurement of what that lever can move, and what it can't.

Three readings that bear on the goal.
  • The goal is recipe-bound, not capacity-bound. Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 has less effective capacity than Snowball (10.0 vs 11.6) and still lands within 2.8 AIME / 3.8 GPQA-D / 2.7 LCBv6 / 1.8 MMLU-Pro of the target, dead level on IFEval. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (10.2) and Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B (10.1) clear the target outright on most of what they report, and Qwen3.5-9B does it with no sparsity at all. Nothing about 67B-A2B's shape rules this target out.
  • Budget ambition per benchmark, not across the board. On one identical base, the reasoning post-train is worth ~18–20 points on AIME and HMMT and ~12 on LiveCodeBench, but only ~4 on GPQA-D and ~1–2 on MMLU-Pro and IFEval. Promise movement where the recipe demonstrably moves things; measure the rest.
  • Two of the seven can't be checked against the field. CFEval is a Qwen house metric — Google publishes a real Codeforces Elo (1718 for Gemma-4-26B-A4B) which is a different number on a different scale, and inclusionAI's “CodeForces(Elo)” is a 0–100 score. HMMT now has three live variants (Feb 25, Nov 25, Feb 26) and vendors pick between them. IFEval barely discriminates either — 85.0–91.9 across everything here, including a 4B dense model. Weight the goal toward GPQA-D, LiveCodeBench v6 and MMLU-Pro, where the field actually reports comparable numbers.
§07

The data on hand

Three corpora we would actually build the recipe from — one public trace corpus we can distill from, our own task pool, and our own trace line. Counts are live, taken from parquet row counts rather than from the cards, because in two of the three cases the card is wrong or stale.

The tension worth naming up front. The goal in §06 is non-agentic — math, science, coding, language. All three corpora here are agentic: terminal tasks graded by a test suite. But most of their content isn't. TaskTrove's two largest directories are nemotron-gym-knowledge-mcqa (616,888 tasks) and nemotron-gym-math-stack-overflow-v2 (436,307) — 46.5% of the pool between them, and neither is agentic in substance. Nemotron Terminal is 61.8% math/code/SWE prompt sets mechanically re-skinned as terminal tasks. Both are reasoning content wearing a terminal costume, with a pass/fail verifier attached. That is an asset for a non-agentic goal, but the reward shape is a test-suite pass, not an answer match — the conversion is not free.

Both views are a complete census — every one of the 38 shards was read, and the 30 sources and 14 teachers each sum exactly to 1,696,847. Two things stand out. 88.7% of the corpus comes from three teachers (GLM-4.7, GPT 5.1 Nano, GLM-4.6), so its failure modes are largely three models' failure modes. And 36.5% of it was generated by a nano-tier teacher — a distilled student inherits its teacher's ceiling, so a third of this corpus carries a low one. If AgentTrove feeds Snowball, the teacher mix is worth deciding deliberately rather than inheriting. 5.4% of rows carry no source label at all. The concentration is the real headline: two sources are 39% of the rows and two teachers are 75% of them, which is a very different corpus from what “219 runs across 30 sources” suggests. Neither breakdown is published anywhere — the card lists its source repos but gives no counts.

There is effectively no success signal in AgentTrove. The result field is 87.1% null, and most of what remains is harness failure rather than task outcome: 199,219 rows (11.7%) are errors, of which AgentTimeoutError alone is 177,268. Only 19,487 rows — 1.15% of the corpus — carry any pass/fail value at all (18,159 success, 1,328 failure), and the encodings are inconsistent across source datasets: success appears as both 1.0 and success, timeouts as both AgentTimeoutError and timeout. The card also documents a reward float column that does not exist — verified against the live 21-column schema, where result is a string. So the §07 question of whether to rejection-sample on reward is partly moot here: for 99% of AgentTrove there is nothing to sample on. That is fixable at generation time, and worth fixing before the next campaign.
A quality problem in our own SFT data, found while pulling these examples. Roughly half of OpenThoughts3 rows never finish: the QwQ-32B response opens <think>, runs to a generation length cap, and stops mid-sentence with no closing tag and no final answer. In a 180-row sample across nine offsets: 70% of math rows, 62% of code rows, 8% of science rows are truncated this way — 46.7% overall. Truncated rows are the long ones (median 53,954 characters against 33,524 for complete rows), and some degenerate into mixed Chinese/English before they stop. The three examples below were chosen from the complete half. This is a sample, not a census — but if OpenThoughts3 is going to feed Snowball's SFT, the real usable row count needs measuring before it is quoted as 1.2M.
The finding in here that should change how we run datagen. NVIDIA ablated trajectory filtering three ways and no filtering won by more than 2×: 12.4 TB2.0 unfiltered, 6.74 keeping only completed trajectories, 5.06 keeping only trajectories that passed their tests. Their reading is that strict filtering “discards over half the available training data,” and that failed trajectories carry real supervision — realistic error states and recovery patterns. Success-only filtering would have cut their synthetic pool from 264,207 to 83,448. If our datagen pipeline rejection-samples on reward, this is a cheap, well-evidenced ablation to run against it before the next campaign.

What Snowball can aim for

Ben's two stated targets assessed against the evidence above, then the bands, the headline, and the risks.

Target 1 — "benchmark comparably to Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Thinking"

AIME / LCB · FEASIBLEGPQA-D · CONDITIONAL ON PRETRAIN

The A3B frontier moved past this target in the last year: Cascade-2 (30B-A3B) is at 92.4 AIME25 and Qwen3.5/3.6-35B-A3B at 92–93 AIME26 — so "comparable to 80B-A3B" now means matching a mid-pack A3B model. By effective capacity (√(total×active)) Snowball ≈ 11.6B-equivalent, more than the two models already exceeding the target (Cascade-2 ≈ 9.5, Qwen3.5-35B ≈ 10.2). ZAYA1 proves active params don't bind competition math (0.7B active → 89 AIME26).

GPQA-Diamond is the exception: it tracks pretraining, not post-training — Cascade-2 reached only 76.1 with the best published RL pipeline, while 84–86 at A3B required Qwen's 2026 corpus. That component depends on Marin's pretrain, so the proposal should promise AIME/LCB movement and measure GPQA, never gate on it.

Target 2 — "match the reward of a stronger teacher (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, GLM-5.2)"

FEASIBLE AS FRACTION-OF-GAPTOKENIZER CONSTRAINT

The A3B class has done the analogous thing: Qwen3-Coder-Next (A3B) matches DeepSeek-V3.2 (A37B — 12× active) on SWE-bench Verified. Frame the goal as closing a stated fraction of the measured teacher-reward gap on a fixed task distribution, with the ID/OOD transfer matrix as the scientific payload — nobody has published that controlled comparison (on-policy distill vs GRPO vs hybrid).

Constraint: logit-level distillation arms need a shared tokenizer — Qwen3.5-122B-A10B works today; GLM-5.2 supports only reward-level matching or trace SFT. And hold the agent harness fixed across arms: cross-scaffold transfer is weak and will swamp the effect.

The headline nobody can pre-empt: first competitive A2B

No published model has a frontier-relative win below 3B active — the entire 2026 efficiency race (Coder-Next, North Mini Code, Nemotron-Lightning, Qwen3.6-35B, Ornith-35B) converged on exactly A3B. Snowball at 2B active and 34× sparsity probes below the empirical floor (max published at ≤4B active is Qwen3-Next's 27×; 33× exists only at 1.6T scale), with 67B of total capacity as the compensating asset. Even reaching the A3B band at A2B is a first — and the routing-stability study underneath it (our P0 workstream) is a standalone contribution, since no open agentic MoE-RL stability recipe exists at any sparsity.

Risks the proposal should name

  • The Ring-mini bracket. The nearest active-count analog (A1.4B) sits at 74 AIME25 while A3B models hit 92 — the anchors bracket every target widely. Recipe quality is the whole ballgame, which is the point of the proposal but also the risk.
  • 34× sparsity is untested at small scale. Routing/expert-utilization stability under RL at 2B active is the genuine technical unknown — hence stability work (routing replay, consistency checklist, telemetry) precedes every recipe experiment.
  • Base quality is outside RL's control. RL elicits, it rarely injects; every band above assumes a competent distilled starting point. Gates should be conditional on the post-SFT checkpoint's arrival and quality.
  • Eval protocol dwarfs recipe deltas. Scaffold choice swings TB2.0 by 2–4× and vendor reasoning numbers assume 32–80k token budgets — one fixed harness, one fixed generation budget, pass@1 and pass@k, every claim.
